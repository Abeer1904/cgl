The Active
Citizenship
Festival

A travelling experiential platform that turns complex civic, social, and policy questions into playable formats for learning, training, and organisational alignment.

What TACF is

Not a conference.
Not a workshop.
A rehearsal space.

Move from awareness to practice. Participants learn by making choices and defending them. Surface incentives, constraints, and trade-offs before they become real-world failures.

TACF is a modular, deployable platform. It works across campuses, institutions, districts, and corporate teams — and can be configured around a single theme or run as a full festival format across a day or multiple days.

Who this is for

Three audiences.
One platform.

TACF is designed as a deployable service for institutions working with complexity, people, and real constraints.

Audience 01
Education

Civic learning, critical thinking, participation, and systems literacy through play-based modules. Designed for schools, colleges, and universities — and for programmes that want learning that sticks.

Audience 02
Government

Preparedness, planning, and citizen-facing engagement through simulations and facilitated labs. For teams that need to rehearse decisions before those decisions cost too much to reverse.

Audience 03
Business

Leadership development, ethics and trade-off literacy, and decision-making under uncertainty. For organisations that need their teams to think in systems, not just execute in processes.

What happens inside

Six modules.
One programme.

Pick a bundle, or curate a programme. Modules can be mixed based on theme, audience, and time.

Module 01
Simulations

Issue-based, time-bound decision environments that force collaboration under constraints. Participants govern, negotiate, and decide — and live with the consequences inside the game.

Module 02
Games Mela

A curated showcase of playable games across climate, governance, gender, and digital systems. Participants move across stations, playing and discussing as they go.

Module 03
Scenario Labs

Facilitated scenario runs that surface trade-offs, incentives, and second-order effects. Designed for teams that need to think together before they act together.

Module 04
Debate Formats

Parliamentary-style formats for argument, listening, and democratic reasoning. Structured disagreement as a civic skill.

Module 05
Media Literacy

Play-based misinformation and media literacy experiences adapted to local contexts. Built around Farzi and other CGL tools.

Module 06
Civic Vocabulary

Short modules to build participation habits and practical civic vocabulary — accessible entry points for audiences new to civic engagement.

Outcomes

What changes
after TACF.

TACF is designed to improve decision quality and participation, not just awareness.

Shared language

Teams and cohorts leave with a common language for complex issues — and a shared experience to reference when decisions get hard.

Cost literacy

Participants learn to articulate costs, consequences, and constraints in decisions — not just intentions and goals.

Systems thinking

People see interdependencies, incentives, and second-order effects — moving from linear cause-and-effect thinking to systemic understanding.

Civic agency

Learners move from opinion to agency — with clearer pathways for action inside the institutions and communities they belong to.

Programme insight

Play creates structured insight you can use for programme design, curriculum development, and learning evaluation.

Alignment

Institutions get faster alignment across roles without flattening disagreement — structured play surfaces tension productively.
