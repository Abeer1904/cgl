About the game

Rights in
the network.

Digital Naagrik — the Digital Citizen — is a civic simulation for navigating the rights, responsibilities, and power dynamics of digital life. Players take on roles as platform users, platform companies, government regulators, and civil society actors — experiencing the governance of the digital public space from multiple perspectives.

The game is built around a conviction that digital citizenship is not primarily about skills — it is about rights, power, and participation. Digital Naagrik builds the civic literacy to understand and claim digital rights, not just use digital tools.

01 — Design

Digital rights
co-design

Built through engagement with digital rights practitioners, platform governance researchers, youth educators, and civil society organisations working on digital literacy and rights. Design workshops were run to identify the specific governance knowledge gaps that young digital citizens face.

The design question: what does it mean to be a citizen in a digital space, not just a user? The game had to make the rights and governance dimensions of digital life tangible — moving from user literacy to civic literacy.

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A civic simulation for understanding digital rights and platform governance from multiple perspectives.

Roles

Platform users, platform companies, government regulators, civil society advocates, and independent journalists — each with distinct rights, powers, and governance stakes.

Platform governance rounds

Players navigate content moderation decisions, data privacy scenarios, and platform accountability contexts — experiencing the governance logic from each role.

Rights framework

A digital rights card set grounds each scenario in specific rights claims — freedom of expression, privacy, data protection, platform accountability — building normative literacy alongside civic understanding.

Format

Facilitated simulation for 10–25 participants. 2.5–3.5 hours. Designed for school and college digital literacy programmes, youth organisations, and government digital awareness campaigns.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Digital Naagrik is deployed in digital literacy programmes, civic education contexts, and government digital awareness initiatives.

School & college

Used in digital literacy and civic education programmes as an experiential introduction to digital citizenship and rights.

Youth organisations

Deployed in youth digital rights and media literacy programmes as a structured exploration of platform governance.

Government campaigns

Run in digital awareness campaigns as an engaging alternative to information-delivery formats — building active citizenship, not just compliance.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants develop digital citizenship — the civic knowledge and agency to navigate digital life as rights-holders, not just users.

Rights vocabulary

Data privacy, freedom of expression, platform accountability, content moderation — terms become civic tools participants can use.

Governance literacy

Players develop understanding of how digital spaces are governed — and by whom — building the knowledge base for civic engagement.

Platform critique

Multi-role play builds critical understanding of platform incentives and power — moving beyond user literacy to structural analysis.

Civic agency

Participants emerge with the sense that digital rights are claimable — and the vocabulary to claim them.
