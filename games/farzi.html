About the game

Why fake
news travels.

Farzi is a community card game about misinformation — not as a moral failing, but as a systemic behaviour. Players experience the incentive logic of sharing false information: the social rewards of being first, the cognitive costs of verification, and the platform dynamics that amplify without checking.

The game was designed in the Indian context — where WhatsApp forwards, video misinformation, and community-level information networks create distinct misinformation dynamics. Farzi builds the systemic literacy needed to understand these patterns and the critical reflexes to navigate them.

01 — Design

Information
ecology research

Built from sustained research into misinformation dynamics in India — how false information travels through family and community networks, what makes people share without verifying, and where the verification systems that could check spread have failed to develop.

The design question: can you create an environment where players experience the systemic logic of misinformation spread without moralising at them? The game had to make bad forwarding behaviour feel rational — and then reveal the collective cost.

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A community card game for 4–12 players. 60–90 minutes. Designed for youth and community media literacy contexts.

Information cards

Content cards mix true information, partially false, and completely fabricated — replicating the real information environment where most content is mostly true, most of the time.

Forwarding incentives

Players score points by forwarding widely-shared content — but lose points when false content they forwarded is fact-checked. The incentive structure mirrors platform dynamics.

Verification mechanics

Players can spend time-tokens to verify content — but verification is costly, and the game calibrates the trade-off to replicate real forwarding behaviour.

Format

Card game for 4–12 players. 60–90 minutes. Designed for youth, community, and media literacy contexts — runs without facilitator expertise.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Farzi is used in media literacy programmes, community education, and digital citizenship contexts across India.

School & college

Used in media literacy and digital citizenship programmes as an experiential introduction to misinformation dynamics.

Community settings

Deployed in community education programmes — particularly effective in contexts with high WhatsApp usage and limited formal media literacy.

Journalist training

Run in journalism and fact-checking training to build intuition for the systemic incentives that drive misinformation spread.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants develop systemic misinformation literacy — understanding why false information spreads, not just that it does.

Systems literacy

Players understand misinformation as a systemic behaviour produced by incentive structures — not just individual gullibility.

Verification habits

The game builds the habit of pausing before forwarding — not through moralising, but through experiencing the cost of not verifying.

Platform critique

Participants develop critical understanding of how platform design amplifies misinformation — building the literacy to demand platform accountability.

Community resilience

Groups emerge with shared vocabulary for discussing misinformation and shared norms for information behaviour in their networks.
