About the game

Why fixes
fail.

Fix It! is a systems-thinking workshop game that puts players inside broken institutional processes. They must identify root causes, design interventions, and model the consequences — while managing the political, resource, and capacity constraints that make governance change difficult.

The game is built around a simple but hard truth: most governance problems are not solved by better policy. They persist because of incentive structures, information asymmetries, and institutional path dependencies that resist straightforward solutions. Fix It! makes these invisible forces visible.

01 — Design

Problem
archaeology

Designed through analysis of persistent governance failures across sectors — public distribution, health delivery, grievance redress, infrastructure maintenance. The game scenarios are drawn from real-system failures documented through field research and practitioner engagement.

The design question: can you create an environment where people experience the logic of a broken system — not just see it from the outside, but feel the constraints that produce failure from the inside?

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A diagnostic and design workshop structured around system failure scenarios.

Scenario cards

Each scenario presents a broken system: a public service that fails to reach beneficiaries, an institution that cannot enforce its own mandate, a process caught in a compliance trap.

Root cause mapping

Players map the failure system — identifying incentives, information flows, and structural constraints before proposing any fix.

Intervention design

Teams design and test interventions — and then stress-test them against second-order effects, political resistance, and resource constraints.

Format

Workshop format for 15–30 participants. 3 hours. Designed for governance reform programmes, IAS and IPS training, and policy design courses.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Fix It! is used in governance training, policy design programmes, and reform workshops.

Civil service training

Used in IAS, IPS, and state civil service training to build systems-thinking capacity alongside technical domain knowledge.

Policy institutes

Run in policy design workshops as a structured method for understanding implementation failure before designing interventions.

Reform programmes

Deployed at the outset of reform processes to create shared diagnosis among stakeholders before committing to solutions.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants leave with a practical systems-thinking toolkit — and humility about what governance change actually requires.

Systems vocabulary

Root cause, feedback loop, incentive structure — participants use these as analytical tools, not just terminology.

Failure empathy

Understanding why broken systems persist builds the political and analytical intelligence needed to change them.

Intervention design

Participants practice designing change that accounts for second-order effects, resistance, and unintended consequences.

Diagnostic skill

Groups develop the habit of mapping a system before proposing a fix — a practitioner skill that transfers across domains.
