About the game

Governing
the village.

Gram Vikas simulates the decision space of a gram panchayat — the elected village government at the base of India's administrative pyramid. Players take on roles of elected members, gram sevak, and community representatives, navigating budget allocation, service delivery, and conflict between competing community needs.

The simulation surfaces a core tension in rural governance: the gap between what panchayats are mandated to do and the resources, capacity, and political space they actually have. Players leave with a working understanding of why local governance fails — and what would need to change for it to work.

01 — Design

Field research
co-design

Built with sustained engagement across gram panchayats in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh. Design workshops were run with elected panches, gram sevaks, PRI representatives, and district administration. The problem statement was co-authored with practitioners who live inside the system daily.

The core research question: what does a gram sabha feel like from the inside? What are the incentives that drive decisions — and what makes participation meaningful versus performative? The answer shaped every mechanic in the game.

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A facilitated simulation for groups of 10–25. Roles are assigned, budget constraints are real, and decisions have consequences that carry forward into the next round.

Roles

Elected panchayat members, gram sevak, SHG representative, youth delegate, and community observer. Each role carries distinct powers, obligations, and information access.

Budget mechanics

Real allocation decisions across water, sanitation, roads, education, and livelihood — with a fixed corpus that cannot cover everything. Prioritisation forces genuine trade-offs.

Gram Sabha round

The simulation replicates the gram sabha structure — agenda, participation, resolution, and the informal negotiations that happen before and after the formal meeting.

Format

Facilitated group play for 10–25 participants. 2.5–4 hours. Designed for Panchayati Raj training programmes, SIRD curricula, and governance reform workshops.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Gram Vikas is designed for institutional deployment through state PRIs, SIRD networks, and development programme partners.

SIRD & PRI Training

Embedded within State Institute of Rural Development curricula for elected representative induction training and capacity building refreshers.

NGO & CSO Partners

Deployed by development organisations working on PRI strengthening, water governance, and local planning processes across India.

Facilitator Training

We train facilitators to run the simulation independently. The facilitation guide, debrief protocols, and reflection tools transfer with the training.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants build practical governance literacy — not as theory but as rehearsed decision experience.

Governance vocabulary

Budget allocation, gram sabha procedure, convergence schemes — terms become tools participants can actually use.

Incentive awareness

Playing elected roles builds empathy for structural constraints that are otherwise invisible to community members and programme staff.

Planning capacity

Participants practice prioritisation, negotiation, and consensus-building in a low-stakes rehearsal environment.

Institutional design

Groups surface what structural changes would make the panchayat system more effective — a starting point for reform conversations.
