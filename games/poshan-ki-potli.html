About the game

Food as
a decision.

Poshan ki Potli — the Nutrition Bag — is a card game for building practical nutrition literacy in community settings. Players work with food cards representing local ingredients and recipes to build balanced meals, explore dietary diversity, and understand the nutritional value of accessible foods.

The game is built around a practical insight: nutrition education that ignores local food systems, economic constraints, and community food culture fails to change behaviour. Poshan ki Potli is grounded in what communities actually eat — building from existing knowledge, not against it.

01 — Design

Community
nutrition co-design

Built through sustained engagement with frontline nutrition workers, ASHA and anganwadi facilitators, and community women across several states. Food cards were developed from local ingredient inventories — ensuring the game reflects actual food availability, not aspirational nutrition models.

The design question: how do you build nutrition knowledge in contexts where generic nutrition education has repeatedly failed? The answer was to start from local food culture and work outwards — building from what people know toward what they need to know.

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A card game for 4–15 players. 60–90 minutes. Designed for community and frontline health worker settings.

Food cards

Each card represents a local ingredient or prepared food, with nutritional information presented visually and in simple text — accessible to participants with low literacy.

Meal building

Players build meals from available food cards — aiming for balance across food groups while working within economic and seasonal constraints.

Recipe challenge

Recipe cards introduce traditional local preparations — building pride in local food culture while surfacing their nutritional value.

Format

Card game for 4–15 players. 60–90 minutes. Designed for ASHA and AWW training, community SHG sessions, and mother-child health programmes.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Poshan ki Potli is deployed through frontline health systems, nutrition programmes, and community health organisations.

ASHA & AWW

Used in frontline health worker training to build nutrition facilitation capacity alongside technical nutrition content.

Mother-child health

Deployed in mother-child nutrition programmes as an engaging alternative to information-delivery formats.

SHG settings

Run in women's self-help group settings as a community nutrition literacy and behaviour change tool.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants build practical nutrition literacy — grounded in local food culture and accessible to low-literacy contexts.

Nutrition vocabulary

Food groups, dietary diversity, macro and micronutrients — concepts become accessible through local food cards and familiar ingredients.

Meal planning skill

Players practise balanced meal planning within real economic and seasonal constraints — building practical decision-making capacity.

Local food confidence

The game builds knowledge of and pride in local food culture — countering the tendency of nutrition programmes to dismiss traditional diets.

Facilitator capacity

Frontline workers gain confidence to conduct nutrition education sessions using the game as a facilitation tool.
