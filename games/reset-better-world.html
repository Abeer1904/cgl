About the game

What would
you rebuild?

Reset / Better World is a futures scenario simulation that asks players to do something difficult: imagine institutional systems from scratch under the pressure of crisis. Starting from a moment of breakdown — ecological, economic, or social — teams design the governance structures they would build for a different world.

The game is built around a conviction: that the best way to understand existing institutions is to imagine building alternatives to them. Players engage with questions of legitimacy, representation, enforcement, and accountability — not as philosophy, but as practical design challenges under constraint.

01 — Design

Futures
methodology

Built from engagement with scenario planning, transition design, and futures studies practitioners. Draws on deep research into historical moments of institutional reset — post-conflict, post-crisis, and post-collapse governance reconstruction — to ground the fictional scenarios in real patterns.

The design question: how do you create a playable environment for institutional imagination — where players design seriously rather than fantasise? The answer required building real constraints into the futures scenario structure.

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A futures simulation structured around a crisis scenario and institutional design challenge.

Scenario setting

Players enter a post-crisis context: climate breakdown, economic collapse, political rupture, or pandemic aftermath. The scenario establishes what has failed and what must be rebuilt.

Institutional design

Teams design governance structures — decision rights, accountability mechanisms, resource allocation systems, and participation models — under time and resource constraints.

Stress testing

Designs are stress-tested: second groups play adversarial roles, testing whether the proposed institutions hold under pressure, capture, and resource scarcity.

Format

Facilitated simulation for 15–30 participants. 3–4 hours. Designed for futures workshops, governance reform convenings, and leadership development programmes.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Reset / Better World is used in futures workshops, governance reform processes, and leadership development contexts.

Futures workshops

Deployed in organisational and policy futures workshops as an experiential alternative to scenario planning presentations.

Reform convenings

Used at the outset of governance reform processes to create shared vision among stakeholders before designing incremental changes.

Leadership development

Run in leadership programmes to build capacity for institutional imagination and change-under-uncertainty.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants develop institutional imagination — the capacity to think seriously about governance design, not just governance critique.

Design vocabulary

Accountability, legitimacy, enforcement, representation — participants use these as practical design criteria, not abstract values.

Transition literacy

Understanding what institutional change actually requires — including what must be given up — builds realistic reform thinking.

Systems design skill

Participants practise designing governance under constraints — a rare capacity in most professional contexts.

Futures confidence

Groups develop comfort with uncertainty and ambiguity as conditions for design, not obstacles to it.
