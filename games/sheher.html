About the game

The city
as decision space.

Sheher puts players inside a mid-sized Indian city. As elected councillors, municipal commissioners, citizen groups, and developers, they navigate the real tensions of urban growth: housing versus green space, infrastructure against affordability, master plans that collide with ground realities.

The simulation is built around the city as a learning environment — a place where governance, planning, finance, and politics intersect in ways that cannot be separated. Players discover why urban decisions are hard precisely because every interest is legitimate.

01 — Design

Research
with cities

Designed through engagement with urban local bodies, town and country planning departments, and civic organisations across Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities. Design workshops were run with municipal councillors, planning officials, RWA leaders, and urban practitioners.

The research question at the core: what does urban governance feel like to the people inside it? How do ward councillors experience resource constraints, public pressure, and political accountability simultaneously?

02 — Development

Gameplay &
mechanics

A multi-stakeholder simulation structured around a city council planning session.

Roles

Mayor, ward councillors, municipal commissioner, developer lobby, civil society coalition, and citizen representatives — each with distinct powers, mandates, and incentives.

City budget

Real municipal finance choices: property tax, central transfers, land monetisation, and user charges. Revenue decisions determine what the city can actually build.

Planning round

Players negotiate a city master plan — zoning, infrastructure, housing policy, and public space. Interests conflict; resolutions require coalition-building.

Format

Facilitated play for 12–30 participants. 3–4 hours. Designed for urban governance training, smart cities workshops, and planning education.

03 — Deployment

How it runs
in the field

Sheher is deployed in urban governance training, planning education, and civic engagement programmes.

Municipal training

Used in urban local body capacity building — from newly elected councillors to senior planning officials.

Planning schools

Integrated into urban planning curricula as an experiential module on governance, politics, and citizen participation.

Civil society convenings

Run as an anchor experience in city-level multi-stakeholder dialogues on urban futures and planning processes.

Outcomes

What changes

Participants build a working intuition for urban governance — what makes it hard and what makes it possible.

Urban vocabulary

Zoning, FAR, municipal finance, devolution — terms become usable through decision experience.

Stakeholder empathy

Playing multiple roles builds understanding of why different actors see the same city differently.

Coalition logic

Players practise the negotiation and compromise that makes urban governance work — and what breaks it.

Planning literacy

Groups develop intuition for why master plans fail to match ground reality — and what planning approaches might work better.
