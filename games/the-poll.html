Overview

Overview.

The Poll is an election simulation developed by Civic Games Lab in 2018. Players assume the role of political parties competing to win seats in the Lok Sabha.

The game takes players through the full arc of democratic politics: understanding constituency-level problems, drafting manifestoes, defending policy choices, allocating resources, campaigning effectively, and responding to opponents' strategies.

At its core, The Poll asks players to understand politics not only as a contest for power, but as a system of problem identification, promise-making, persuasion, resource management, and accountability.

The game has been reported on by national and international media, and became one of Civic Games Lab's foundational public-facing simulations.

What the game does

What the game does.

Players begin by studying the problems of different constituencies. They then draft manifestoes based on the issues they choose to prioritise. These manifestoes must be defended through argumentation — players explain why their proposed solutions are appropriate for the constituencies they are trying to win.

The game then moves into campaign strategy. Players use money, campaign tools, media influence, and political tactics to expand their presence, undercut opponents, and win constituencies.

By turning elections into a playable system, The Poll helps players understand how political parties make promises, frame issues, mobilise resources, and compete for public trust.

Phase 01
Understand the
Constituency

Players study constituency cards built from real issues across Indian seats — bellwether seats, dynastic seats, minority-concentrated seats, and seats reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

Phase 02
Draft the
Manifesto

Using solution cards drawn from three election cycles of real party manifestoes, players draft their policy positions — choosing which promises to make and how to map solutions to constituency problems.

Phase 03
Defend the
Manifesto

Players defend positions to other parties and to voters. Challengers can question, attack, and undercut. The room becomes a live public argument about what governance should look like.

Phase 04
Campaign and
Win

Players spend money on campaign strategies — door-to-door, star campaigners, WhatsApp forwards, booth capturing, horse trading. The strategies that work in the game are the strategies that work in real Indian elections.

Research methodology

Research methodology.

Each component of The Poll was developed through research, field interviews, iterative design, and continuous testing. Nothing in the game is invented — every card, problem, and strategy is grounded in documented electoral reality.

Layer 01
Constituency
Cards

The Constituency Cards were designed to reflect real issues across Indian constituencies. The team mined newspaper articles, electoral data, and interviews with local organisations to identify constituency-level concerns.

Constituencies were selected using publicly available data and grouped into categories: bellwether seats, dynastic seats, minority-concentrated seats, and seats reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.

Layer 02
Solution
Cards

The Solution Cards were developed by studying national and state-level political party manifestoes across three election cycles. These were analysed and organised into the core areas around which elections are commonly fought — infrastructure, employment, health, public services, welfare, and governance.

These solution areas were then mapped to the problem statements across constituency cards, creating a evidence-based correspondence between what voters need and what parties can promise.

Layer 03
Campaign
Strategy
Cards

The Campaign Strategy Cards were based on common electioneering practices used by political parties — including legitimate strategies such as star campaigners and door-to-door canvassing, as well as darker political practices such as booth capturing, horse trading, and digital misinformation through WhatsApp forwards.

The game structure was deliberately broken into phases: understanding problems, drafting manifestoes, defending solutions, and campaigning for electoral success.

Rollout

A national
tour.

Civic Games Lab took The Poll on a national tour across India. The game was played in more than ten states, across educational institutions, civil society organisations, political spaces, college competitions, and informal living-room sessions.

The rollout helped build an entire value chain around the game — from gameplay sessions and public conversations to competitions, media coverage, and civic education workshops. The game travelled across regions including Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, and Tamil Nadu.

Educational institutions

Schools, colleges, and universities across ten states. College competitions were built into the rollout as structured public events, with full tournament formats.

Civil society & political spaces

Civil society organisations, political parties, and advocacy groups — The Poll was one of the few simulations played by political actors themselves, not only audiences studying politics.

Living rooms and workshops

Informal home sessions alongside public workshop formats — the game was designed to be accessible in both contexts, without requiring a professional facilitator for every run.

Reach & impact

Reach and impact.

The Poll sold 500 copies and reached more than 40,000 players through facilitated sessions, workshops, competitions, and independent gameplay.

Its wider significance lies in how it helped Civic Games Lab develop a repeatable methodology for governance games: start with real public problems, translate them into playable systems, create space for argumentation, and allow players to test how choices, resources, and strategies affect outcomes.

In many ways, The Poll became the intellectual spine of Civic Games Lab's later governance simulations.
